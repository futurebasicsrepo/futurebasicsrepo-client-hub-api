# Physical product development agreement: the standard wording

This is what a physical product agreement says when it is made with the defaults (the example below is a hat and apparel brand with an electronics item, production ordered by us from the factory). The editor can change any clause per contract. It is a starting point written in plain English, not legal advice: have a lawyer read it before the first one goes out.

Words that change per contract: the parties, scope, dates, fee and payment schedule, the product categories (compliance wording), the number of sample rounds, the shipping terms, the inspection standard, and who places production orders.

## 1. Parties

This agreement is made on November 1, 2026 between Future Basics ("Future Basics"), 1134 Sansom St, Philadelphia, PA 19107, and Trail Hat Co (the "Client"), 5 Brim St, Portland OR. It covers the product development project described below (the "Project"): Trail hat and cap line.

## 2. Scope of work

Future Basics will carry out the following development work for the Client. Anything not listed here is outside this agreement and can be added through a change request.

- A complete tech pack for each style
- Sourcing and quotes from up to three factories
- Up to two rounds of samples per style

## 3. Timeline

The dates below are our best plan, not a guarantee. They hold as long as the Client gives content, approvals and feedback within 5 business days of each request. They also depend on factory sampling and production times, materials, customs and freight, which are outside our control. Time the Client takes, or a delay outside our control, moves the dates by the same amount.

- Agreement date: November 1, 2026
- Work begins: November 3, 2026
- Estimated final sample approval: January 15, 2027

## 4. Fixed fee

The fee is a fixed price for our development work described in the scope above: design, tech packs, sampling management and sourcing. It does not include the cost of samples, materials, tooling, production, inspection, testing, certification, freight, duties or taxes unless the scope says it does, and it does not change because the work takes us more or less time than we planned.

**$9,000.00**

## 5. Payment schedule

We email an invoice for each payment on its due date, with a link to pay it online. Each invoice is due within the terms printed on it.

**$4,500.00 on signing, $4,500.00 on final sample approval**

- Deposit: $4,500.00, On signing
- Balance: $4,500.00, On final sample approval

## 6. Changes to the scope

If the Client wants something that is not in the scope, either party can propose a change in writing (email is fine). We say what it adds in price and time, and the change only counts once the Client agrees to it in writing. Work already done on an approved design or sample is not redone for free because a preference changed.

## 7. Samples and approval

The fixed fee includes up to 2 rounds of samples. A round is one set of samples made from the tech pack and the Client's feedback on the round before it. Extra rounds, and the cost of the samples themselves, materials and shipping, are quoted before we order them unless the scope says they are included. The Client approves each sample, or lists the changes it wants, in writing within 5 business days. The sample the Client approves in writing, together with the tech pack, is the standard that production is made and inspected against (the "approved sample"). Production does not start until the Client has approved a pre-production sample, and a change after that approval can change the price and the dates.

## 8. Production runs

Production runs are not part of the fixed fee. Future Basics places each production order with the factory itself and supplies the goods to the Client. Each run is confirmed by a written order that we send and the Client approves in writing (email is fine). It states the quantity, the unit price the Client pays, the deposit, the lead time and the shipping terms, and once approved it forms part of this agreement. Unless the order says otherwise, the Client pays a 50% deposit before we place the order with the factory and the balance before the goods ship. The goods become the Client's when it has paid for them in full. Goods made to the Client's design cannot be cancelled or changed once materials are ordered or cutting has begun: the Client pays for the materials bought and the work done up to the time we are told. A quoted price can change if materials, tariffs or exchange rates move before the order is approved.

The Client pays the price on the order and deals only with us. What we pay the factory, our factory quotations and our margin are our own business and are not shown to the Client, and the Client has no right to see them.

Because we supply the goods, we answer to the Client for them: goods that do not match the approved sample and the order are reworked, replaced or credited by us, whatever arrangement we have with the factory. For goods we supply, our total liability for a defect is limited to the price the Client paid for the goods affected, in place of the limit under Limit of liability.

## 9. Quality and inspection

Production is checked against the approved sample and the tech pack. Unless an order says otherwise, finished goods are inspected before they ship, to an acceptable quality level of 2.5 for major defects and 4.0 for minor defects. We arrange the inspection, either ourselves or through a third-party inspector whose cost we quote first, and we share the report. Goods that fail are reworked or remade by the factory before they ship, unless the Client approves the difference in writing. A defect the inspection report did not show must be reported in writing, with photos, within 30 days of the goods arriving. Who answers to the Client for defects in the goods is set out under Production runs.

## 10. What the Client provides

The Client provides the artwork, logos, content, specifications and decisions we ask for, on time, and confirms that it has the right to use everything it gives us. The Client names one person who can approve work and samples on its behalf. If the Client goes quiet for more than 10 days, or a payment is more than 10 days late, we may pause the work until things are back on track and we will tell the Client in writing first.

## 11. Where the product is sold

The Client decides where the product will be sold and is responsible for it meeting the legal requirements there. That includes fibre content and country-of-origin labels, and for children's products the small-part and lead rules; fibre content, care and country-of-origin labels, and for children's clothing the safety rules on flammability, drawcords and small parts; electrical-safety and radio-frequency approvals for each market it is sold in (for example FCC, UL, CE and UKCA), restricted-substance rules such as RoHS, and the packing and shipping rules for lithium batteries. We flag requirements we know of, and we put what the Client tells us on the tech pack and on the factory's order, but we are not a testing laboratory or a certification body and we do not give legal advice. Testing and certification are paid for by the Client, and a product is not approved for sale just because it was made or shipped.

## 12. Who owns the work

When the Client has paid every payment in this agreement, it owns the designs, patterns, tech packs, artwork and other custom work we create for it under this agreement. Future Basics keeps ownership of its own tools, templates, base patterns and know-how, and of anything that existed before the Project or is used across clients, and gives the Client a permanent license to use them as part of the finished product. Tooling, molds and lasts paid for by the Client belong to the Client, even when the factory holds them, and we will help arrange their transfer. Samples belong to the Client once they are paid for. The Client confirms that it owns or may use the logos, names and artwork it gives us, and covers claims that this material infringes someone else's rights. Future Basics may show photos of the finished product in its portfolio unless the Client asks us not to in writing.

## 13. Shipping, duties and risk

Unless an order says otherwise, goods are shipped on FOB (free on board: the goods are the Client's responsibility once loaded at the port of departure) terms. Freight, insurance, import duties, taxes and customs fees are the Client's cost unless an order says they are included, and the Client is the importer of record unless we agree otherwise in writing. Risk of loss or damage passes at the point named in those terms, and we recommend that the Client insures the goods in transit. Dates for production and shipping are estimates: delays by factories, carriers, customs, weather or other causes outside our control are not a breach by us.

## 14. Confidentiality

Each party keeps the other's non-public business information private, uses it only for this Project, and returns or deletes it on request when the Project ends. The Client's designs and plans are shared with factories only as far as needed to make samples and goods, and we ask factories to keep them private. Our supplier details and prices are our confidential information too. This does not cover information that is already public, that the party already knew, or that the law requires it to disclose.

## 15. Our promise, and its limits

We will do the work with the care and skill expected of a professional product development studio. If the Client reports a defect in what we delivered (tech packs, patterns, specifications) within 30 days of delivery, we fix it at no charge. After that, fixes are quoted as new work. We do not promise sales or other commercial results, that a product will pass any test, approval or certification, that it can be sold in any particular country, or that a factory will meet its dates. Factories are independent businesses: our part is to choose them carefully, brief them well, follow the order and inspect the goods, and who answers to the Client for the goods is set out under Production runs.

## 16. Limit of liability

Neither party is liable to the other for lost profits, lost sales, lost data or any indirect or consequential loss. Each party's total liability under this agreement is limited to the fees the Client has paid us under it for development work. This does not limit anything the law does not allow to be limited. The Client is responsible for the product it sells under its own name, including claims from its customers, recalls and action by regulators, except to the extent they are caused by our breach of this agreement, and should carry product liability insurance before it sells the product.

## 17. Ending the agreement

Either party can end this agreement by writing to the other if the other breaks it in a serious way and does not fix it within 10 days of being told. The Client can also end it at any time by writing to us. In either case the Client pays for the work done up to the date of ending, for samples and materials already ordered, and for any production order already placed or in progress, and fees already paid for work done are not refunded. Sections on ownership, confidentiality, liability and payment of money owed continue after the agreement ends.

## 18. General

This is the whole agreement between us about the Project and replaces anything said or written before it. It can only be changed in writing that both parties accept. Neither party can hand this agreement to someone else without the other's written consent, except to a successor of its whole business. If a part of it cannot be enforced, the rest still applies. It is governed by the laws of Pennsylvania. Both parties agree that signing electronically has the same effect as signing on paper, that this agreement can be signed in separate copies, and that the signed electronic copy is the original.

## Production runs when the client orders directly

When "The client orders from the factory directly" is chosen instead, the Production runs clause reads:

> Production runs are not part of the fixed fee. When the Client is ready to produce, the Client places each order directly with the factory on the factory's written quotation and pays the factory directly under the factory's terms. Future Basics is not the manufacturer and is not a party to the factory's contract. As part of this agreement we give the factory the approved tech pack and the approved sample requirements, follow each order through production, arrange the inspection described below, and act for the Client in good faith to settle any problem that comes up. We do not guarantee the factory's work, prices or dates.

When "Not covered by this agreement" is chosen, the Production runs clause is left out.
