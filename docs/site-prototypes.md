# Site prototypes (SW and site prototyping)

`/prototypes` (menu: Sales → Site prototypes). Paste the address of a small business's site and the platform reads it, scores it, builds a new high-standard site for their trade, checks it against a standard, and publishes it onto the Builds showcase to share. Aimed at quick-turn leads in blue-collar trades (plumbing, electrical, HVAC, roofing, carpentry and remodeling, landscaping, painting, cleaning) and home health care.

## The flow
1. **Audit (free, no AI).** Fetches the home page and up to three more (services, about, contact, areas) and scores the site out of 100: not built for phones, no https, phone number not tap-to-call, no quote form, no reviews or licence shown, outdated markup, a stale © year, slow, weak title and search description, no local-business data, and so on. Each finding comes with a sentence a rep can say to the owner, and the site is marked **quick turn** (small, no shop or portal) or a bigger job. It also names the trade.
2. **Build.** The brief is made from what the site says (name, phone, services, hours, colours, pictures) plus the trade's playbook (`src/proto-industry.js`): the sections a visitor in that trade expects, the proof that earns trust, the tone, and the interactive features for the trade (an emergency panel, a "what is wrong?" picker, a do-you-serve-me checker, an instant estimate, before and after, a care needs quiz for home health, colour visualizers, and more). Claude (`claude-opus-5-5`, streamed) builds one self-contained HTML page to the standard in `src/proto-build.js` (mobile first, tap-to-call everywhere, accessible, local-search ready, under 450 KB, motion that sets the work apart: animated hero, scroll reveals, count-ups, responsive buttons, all switched off for reduced motion). With no Claude connected it uses a standard template, and says so.
3. **Honesty.** Only facts from their site are shown as facts. Anything else the page needs (sample reviews, ballpark prices, counts) must be marked `data-illustrative` and shows a small "Sample" tag; the page lists them as "confirm with the owner". Their own pictures can be hot-linked and the page says when it does.
4. **Quality gate.** 27 checks, most of them required: doctype, language, viewport, title and description, exactly one heading, landmarks, size, no outside scripts (every inline script must compile), no outside stylesheets, forms that never submit, alt text, labelled fields, reduced-motion, motion, an interactive feature, tap-to-call, the phone number as given, local-business data, no placeholder text. Safe fixes are applied automatically (`autofix`); a failed required check triggers one repair pass; if that still fails the template is used. A page that fails cannot be published.
5. **Publish and share.** Publishing puts the page at `/p/<slug>` (an unguessable address) and creates a **Builds** entry (hidden until shared; sector, summary and highlights filled in). **Share** invites the owner's email to that build (the showcase emails them a sign-in code) or copies the link. Taking it down ends the link at once and hides the build; deleting archives the build.

## The page is untrusted, so it is sandboxed
A published prototype is markup and script made by a model. It is served with a CSP that sandboxes it into its own origin, allows no network calls (`connect-src 'none'`) and no form submissions, allows only pictures, fonts and its own inline code, and with `noindex`, `no-referrer` and a "Prototype · Future Basics" ribbon. The staff preview runs in a sandboxed iframe too.

## Reading other people's sites safely
Only http and https on the normal ports; every address (and every redirect) is resolved and refused if it points at a private, loopback, link-local or metadata address; responses are capped at 1.5 MB and 12 seconds. Staff only.

## The studio: building a prototype step by step
A prototype is not a one-shot. Open one and the **Studio** panel is where it gets shaped, the way we build here: say what should change in plain words ("make the hero bolder", "add a gallery of past work", "add a 24/7 emergency banner"), or start from one of the ideas, and Claude revises the page that is current. The change is checked against the same quality standard (and repaired once if it breaks something); a change that would make the page worse than it was is **not applied**, and the reason is shown so it can be reworded. Earlier changes in the session are passed along as context, and Claude is told to change only what was asked so the page does not drift.

Every build, rebuild, change and "go back" is a saved **version** (v1, v2, …) with what was asked, what headings it added or removed, whether it passes the check, and what it cost. Any version can be looked at in the preview, and **Go back to this** makes it the current page as a *new* version, so nothing is ever lost. The newest 40 are kept.

**Nothing goes live until it is published.** The public link (`/p/<slug>`) serves the *live* version only: changes and restores wait as "Version N is not live yet" until **Publish version N**, so a prototype that is already in front of an owner never changes under them mid-iteration. Rebuilds work the same way.

It all runs on what is already set up: the Railway service builds and serves the pages (no new hosting), Postgres keeps the versions (`prototype_versions`), the Builds showcase entry and its invites are the share path, and Claude is the same `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`. Without that key the studio says so (and rebuild, go back and publish still work). Limits: `PROTOTYPE_EDITS_PER_DAY` (default 80), 40 changes an hour per person. API: `POST …/:id/edit { instruction }`, `GET …/:id/versions`, `GET …/:id/versions/:n/preview`, `POST …/:id/versions/:n/restore`, `POST …/:id/publish` (publishes the current version). Journeys: J148 (API), J149 (browser). Journey-only switches: `PROTOTYPE_TEST_EDIT`, `PROTOTYPE_TEST_EDIT_MS` (a stand-in that adds the instruction as a section).

## Finding leads
The **Find leads** tab on the page is how a rep finds who to build for. Pick a trade (plumbing, electrician, HVAC, roofing, carpentry, home health, landscaping, painting, cleaning, or any trade) and a town, and the service runs two web searches through Brave (`BRAVE_API_KEY`, the same key Sales Intelligence uses), drops directories and review sites (Yelp, Angi, Facebook, the BBB, franchise pages), merges repeats, skips businesses already in the CRM or already built, and does a light read of each site (up to four at a time, capped at 18 sites a search). Results come back ranked: tired sites that are **quick turns** first, worst score first, then bigger jobs, then sites that were down. Each card shows the business name, the score, the top two problems, the phone, the town, and Build prototype, Add to CRM and Visit site.

**What counts as worth a call.** Only sites that are not already good (a score under 80) and are the right trade get a card, and only those count as leads and quick turns (in the header, on saved searches and on the work home). Everything else is folded into "N sites that already look fine or are another trade", each with the reason, and a **Build anyway** button. A site that could not be read stays in the main list, last of those, because a broken site is a lead too. Rankings and lists ("Top 10 plumbers in Philadelphia", "best … near me", magazines and review sites) are dropped with the directories. Names are the business's own: structured data, then the site name, then the title or first heading, skipping anything that reads like a search phrase ("Expert Plumbing Services In Philadelphia"); when nothing does, the address itself is shown. A plumber who also does heating still counts as a plumber (related trades match).

No search key, or searches used up for the day (`PROTOTYPE_SEARCHES_PER_DAY`, default 30; 15 an hour per person)? Paste up to 25 addresses instead: they are audited the same way and no search is used.

From a card: **Add to the CRM** creates the company, a contact (named for the business until someone learns the owner's name, source `site-prototype`) and a deal in New, with what is wrong with their site on the timeline. Adding the same lead twice changes nothing. **Build the prototype** does that first, then builds, so the prototype is tied to the contact and the deal: the timeline notes when it is ready, when it is published and when it is shared, and sharing moves the deal to Contacted.

### Saved searches (the lead finder on its own)
In **Find leads**, **Saved searches** keeps a trade and a town (up to 10) and runs them by themselves: every day, 3 days, week (the default), 2 weeks or month. The job (**Lead search schedules**, checked every 3 hours, `PROTOTYPE_SCHEDULE_TICK_MS` only shortens it for the journeys) runs the ones that are due, a few at a time, and leaves a few of the day's searches free for people. Each run brings **new sites only**: what an earlier run of the same saved search already showed, and what is in the CRM or already built, is skipped. The saved search card shows when it last ran and "N quick-turn of M", with **new** until someone opens it. It also needs `BRAVE_API_KEY`. The cap is the same `PROTOTYPE_SEARCHES_PER_DAY`.

The best results reach the people who need them: a saved search that found quick-turn sites nobody has opened yet is a **New leads** row on the work home's Today list; the row opens those results (`/prototypes?search=<id>`) and leaves the list once opened. Pause, Resume, Run now and Remove are on the card (API: `…/leads/schedules`, `…/leads/schedules/:id` (PATCH, DELETE), `…/leads/schedules/:id/run`). Journeys J151 (API, including the job running a due search by itself) and J152 (browser).

### On the Platform page
Two cards under Sales: **Site prototypes** (is Claude connected, builds that keep failing or hang for over 20 minutes, builds and changes in the last 7 days with the Claude spend) and **Lead search (Brave)** (key set, searches today of the cap, how the real searches have gone, saved searches and the job behind them). Both are judged from what was stored: a check never spends Brave or Claude quota. The Platform settings list also says whether `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` and `BRAVE_API_KEY` are set.

## Limits and cost
`PROTOTYPES_PER_DAY` (default 25), `PROTOTYPE_CONCURRENCY` (default 2 at a time), 40 builds and 60 audits per person per hour. Each build shows its cost (`PROTOTYPE_PRICE` is the list price per million tokens in and out, default `5,25`). Searches: `BRAVE_API_KEY`, `PROTOTYPE_SEARCHES_PER_DAY`. Other settings: `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, `PROTOTYPE_MODEL`, `BUILDS_PUBLIC_URL` (default `https://builds.thefuturebasics.com`).

## Data and API
Tables `prototypes`, `prototype_versions`, `prototype_searches` and `prototype_search_schedules` (`src/db.js`; a search keeps its ranked results so the page can reopen it): the facts, audit, brief, quality result, the page, the previous page after a rebuild, tokens and cost, views, and the build it is on. Pure rules: `src/proto-crawl.js`, `src/proto-industry.js`, `src/proto-build.js`, `src/proto-leads.js` (`test/proto.test.js`, `test/proto-build.test.js`). Routes: `src/proto-routes.js` (`/v1/admin/prototypes…` including `leads/meta`, `leads/search`, `leads/searches`, `leads/add`; and `/p/:slug`). Journeys: J144 (build), J145 (page), J146 and J147 (leads), J151 and J152 (saved searches).
