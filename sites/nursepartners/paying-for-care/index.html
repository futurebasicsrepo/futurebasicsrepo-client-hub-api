2 · LTC policy checklist

Find these six things on the policy tonight.

Long-term care policies are long and confusing, but six answers decide what it pays. Pull out the policy (or the "schedule of benefits" page) and fill this in. Bring it to the assessment and we'll open the claim with you.

Can't find the policy? Call the insurer's claims line with the policyholder present (or with power of attorney) and ask for a copy and a "benefit summary".

Older policies sometimes cover only nursing homes, or pay a smaller share for home care (e.g. 50% of the facility benefit).

The most it pays per day (or month). This is the number in the calculator above.

How long before benefits start, commonly 90 days. Note whether it counts calendar days or service days; service days take longer if care is part-time.

Usually needing help with 2 of 6 daily activities (bathing, dressing, eating, toileting, transferring, continence) or a severe cognitive impairment, certified by a licensed health practitioner.

The lifetime maximum (or benefit period, like "3 years"), and whether the daily benefit has grown with inflation since purchase.

Most policies require a licensed home care agency (we're licensed by the PA Department of Health), and some pay you directly while others pay the agency.

4 · Every way to pay

The plain-English guide.

Private pay

Most families start here, often from savings, pensions or the sale of a home. You pay by the hour and can adjust hours any time. We take major cards (3% surcharge on credit) and can automate weekly payments.

Worth knowing: home care costs can be a deductible medical expense for the person receiving care, or for a child who claims a parent as a dependent. Ask your tax preparer.

Long-term care insurance

If there's a policy, it's usually the biggest help. Once the benefit trigger is met and the elimination period has passed, it pays up to the daily benefit. We accept assignment of benefits, so the insurer pays us directly, and we handle monthly claim paperwork. You're billed only for what the policy doesn't cover.

Start the claim as early as possible; the elimination period clock usually starts once the trigger is certified.

VA Aid & Attendance

A monthly pension add-on for wartime veterans and surviving spouses who need help with daily activities, or who are housebound. For December 2025 – November 2026, the maximum is about $2,424/month for a single veteran, about $2,875 for a veteran with a spouse, and about $1,557 for a surviving spouse. The net-worth limit (assets plus annual income) is $163,699, with a 36-month look-back on asset transfers.

Because unreimbursed care costs reduce countable income, families paying for home care often qualify for more than they expect. Apply with VA Form 21-2680 and the pension form, or with help from an accredited Veterans Service Officer (free).

Pennsylvania Medicaid: Community HealthChoices

Pennsylvania's Medicaid waiver can pay for in-home personal care for people 21+ who need a nursing-home level of care. For 2026, income must be under about $2,982/month and countable assets under $8,000, with a 5-year look-back on transfers. The home is generally exempt.

NursePartners is a private-pay and LTC insurance agency. If the waiver is your better path, we'll tell you so and point you to the PA Independent Enrollment Broker at 1-877-550-4227.

LIFE (Pennsylvania's PACE program)

For people 55+ who need a nursing-home level of care and live in a LIFE provider's service area. LIFE coordinates medical care, day-center visits and some in-home help. It works best for people already on Medicaid, or who can pay the monthly premium privately.

Medicare

Medicare does not pay for ongoing personal or companion care. It covers short-term, skilled home health (nursing or therapy visits) when someone is homebound after an illness or hospital stay. Many families combine the two: Medicare's skilled visits plus our carepartners for the hours in between.

Getting expert help

For VA and Medicaid planning, an elder law attorney or accredited VA representative is worth the call, especially before moving any assets. We're glad to introduce families to people we trust.
