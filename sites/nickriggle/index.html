About

A philosopher of beauty who learned to fall first.

Nick Riggle left high school to skate professionally, touring the world in demos, shows, and competitions, including three ESPN X Games. Then he went to Berkeley, then NYU, and came out the other side asking what beauty, style, and awesomeness actually demand of us.

He is Professor of Philosophy at the University of San Diego, where his research centers on aesthetic value: what it is, why it matters, and how a life shaped by it might be more free. He works in aesthetics, value theory, philosophy of language, social philosophy, and Kant and post-Kantian philosophy. He received his PhD from New York University in 2013 and his BA, with highest honors, from the University of California, Berkeley.

His academic writing appears in The Philosophical Review, Mind, Philosophy and Phenomenological Research, Philosophers’ Imprint, The Philosophical Quarterly, the British Journal of Aesthetics, and the Journal of Aesthetics and Art Criticism. His essays for general readers appear in The New York Times, Aeon, and Psyche.

He founded and directs the Southern California Aesthetics Forum, co-founded the Food Studies Minor at USD, and co-organized the first major academic conference on the philosophy of street art.

Research

Five threads, argued across fifteen years.

Open any thread to see the papers behind it. Every paper is available as a talk.

Against the picture of taste as private pleasure, Riggle argues that aesthetic value lives in a practice: valuing beauty is a way of sharing attention, building community, and expressing individuality at once. He calls the view aesthetic communitarianism.

  • Aesthetic Value and the Practice of Aesthetic ValuingThe Philosophical Review 133:2 · 2024 · Arthur Danto Prize · Philosopher’s Annual
  • Autonomy and Aesthetic ValuingPhilosophy and Phenomenological Research 109:1 · 2024
  • Aesthetic Acquaintance in PracticeThe Philosophical Quarterly · forthcoming
  • Toward a Communitarian Theory of Aesthetic ValueJournal of Aesthetics and Art Criticism 80:1 · 2022
  • Community and Conflict in Aesthetic Life: A Reply to SongJournal of Aesthetics and Art Criticism 83:2 · 2025
  • Convergence, Community, and Force in Aesthetic DiscourseErgo 8:47 · 2022

Reading Schiller with Samantha Matherne, and Kant against the grain, Riggle develops the thought that our interest in beauty is an interest in a distinctive kind of freedom, and that beauty is bound up with love and happiness.

  • Schiller on Freedom and Aesthetic Value, Parts I & IIwith Samantha Matherne · British Journal of Aesthetics 60:4 and 61:1 · 2020, 2021 · Oxford’s Best of 2020 in Philosophy
  • Connecting Beauty and Lovein Art and Philosophy: New Essays at the Intersection, ed. Alex King · Oxford University Press · 2025
  • Beauty and Happinessin Reasons and Relevance, ed. Steven Hales · Hackett · forthcoming
  • On the Interest in Beauty and DisinterestPhilosophers’ Imprint 16:9 · 2016
  • On the Aesthetic IdealBritish Journal of Aesthetics 55:4 · 2015

Having style, personal or artistic, is a matter of expressing the ideals you hold for yourself or your work. The same thought powers the theory of awesomeness, and a view of how we become new people.

  • Artistic Style as the Expression of Idealswith Robert Hopkins · Philosophers’ Imprint 21:8 · 2021
  • Personal Ideals as MetaphorsJournal of the American Philosophical Association 3:3 · 2018 · Author-meets-critics, Pacific APA 2019
  • Transformative Expressionin Becoming Someone New, eds. Schwenkler & Lambert · Oxford University Press · 2020
  • Personal Style and Artistic StyleThe Philosophical Quarterly 65:261 · 2015
  • Practice, Play, and Style in Alva Noë’s The EntanglementAnalysis · forthcoming

Street art uses the street as an artistic resource, not just a location. Riggle’s 2010 paper set the terms of the philosophical debate. He co-organized the first major conference on the subject in 2015 and returned to it as a keynote in 2025.

  • Street Art: The Transfiguration of the CommonplacesJournal of Aesthetics and Art Criticism 68:3 · 2010
  • Using the Street for Art: A Reply to BaldiniJournal of Aesthetics and Art Criticism 74:2 · 2016
  • Street Art and GraffitiOxford Encyclopedia of Aesthetics, ed. Michael Kelly · 2014
  • Street Art as Aesthetic Practice: Community, Conflict, CritiqueKeynote · Painting the Town, Georgia State University · 2025

What are we doing when we give a wine 94 points, hype a record, or rank the canon? Recent work on aesthetic discourse, ranking, and snobbery, heading toward a trade book on taste in the era of content.

  • Can’t Kill the Vibe: Against Hope in Aesthetic DiscourseMind 135:1 · 2026

Teaching

Philosophy, taught like it matters.

At the University of San Diego, Nick teaches courses in aesthetics, ethics, and the philosophy of art. The seminar room works the way a skate session does: everyone is trying something hard, in public, and getting better because of who is watching.

Expect close reading, real argument, and writing that gets rewritten. Expect, too, to leave with a sharper sense of what you find beautiful and why that is not a small thing.

He also directs and co-founded the USD Food Studies Minor, an interdisciplinary program on how we grow, cook, share, and judge what we eat.
