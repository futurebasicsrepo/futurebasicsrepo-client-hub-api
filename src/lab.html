How to start

  1. On an iPad, keep it on the home screen. In Safari tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. Open the Design Lab icon and sign in once with your email; a code arrives by email. The Apple Pencil draws, pressing harder draws wider, and fingers move and pinch the view.
  2. Plan a line, or start from a concept. Line plans hold the pieces, prices and margins; each piece gets concepts drawn from it.
  3. Design each piece in one place. Open a concept in the Studio: its picture as layers, and its measured flats, front and back, or side by side. Graphics placed on either side are the same graphic, in inches on the garment.
  4. Simple or Pro. Simple keeps the essentials; Pro adds distress, warp, the ruler, separations, points of measure and grading.
  5. Follow the Checklist to the tech pack. It says what is left (the picture, the flats, the fabric, the approval) and where to fix it, then sends it to a tech pack. Send that to your own factory, or ask Future Basics to make it.

Line plans

An assortment for a season: which pieces, in which role, at what price, with an estimated landed cost and a starting quantity. The assistant builds a first draft from the library and the brand code; you change anything. Landed cost is a share of the shelf price until a concept becomes a real tech pack, where the bill of materials gives the true number.

Concepts

Our own take on each planned piece: a picture, a specific twist, and a check against what inspired it. Look at a concept beside the pieces it was drawn from before you approve it. An approved concept becomes a product in the Future Basics room and its tech pack is drafted from it.

Brands we watch

The Lab reads each one on its own every few days. It follows each site's robots.txt, goes slowly, and when a site says no, or sits behind a bot wall, it finds the brand's pieces through ordinary web and image search instead.

What the Lab has learned from the board

Every approval, rejection and client verdict is counted: the fabrics, shapes, colours, details and price bands we keep choosing, and the ones we turn down. The assistant reads this before it designs concepts or changes a range. Nothing hidden: these are the counts.
